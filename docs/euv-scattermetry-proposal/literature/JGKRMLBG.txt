EUV reflectometry and scatterometry for thin layer and
periodic structure characterization
Tao Shen1, Iacopo Mochi1, Paolo Ansuinelli1, Dongmin Jeong2, Jinho Ahn2, and Yasin Ekinci1
1Paul Scherrer Institute, Villigen PSI, Switzerland 2Hanyang University, South Korea
ABSTRACT
Non-destructive nano-metrology is a fundamental tool for semiconductor device manufacturing. Practically, a combination of several metrology techniques is needed throughout the fabrication process of semiconductor chips, from EUV photomask inspection to patterned wafer metrology. EUV light not only facilitates the production of smaller features in lithography but also emerges as a powerful approach for the metrology required to characterize and analyze these intricate features in future technology nodes, thanks to its short wavelength, relevant penetration depth, and high reflectivity with relatively high grazing angles compared to that of X-rays. The reflective grazing incidence nanoscope for EUV (REGINE) at the Swiss Light Source is a prototype platform for reflectometry, scatterometry, and coherent diffraction imaging. It operates in the soft X-ray and EUV region (80 to 200 eV photon energy), and covers a grazing incidence angle range of 0◦ to 28.6◦. REGINE provides the possibility to perform grazing incidence reflectometry and scatterometry for semiconductor metrology. In this study, we present the results of optical constants (n and k) determination and layer thickness characterization with reflectometry procedure for an EUV attenuated phase shifting mask blank, and the analysis of scatterometry results of line-space grating profile reconstruction and overlay measurement.
Keywords: REGINE, EUV metrology, Reflectometry, EUV photomask, Scatterometry
1. INTRODUCTION
In modern semiconductor metrology, Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) reflectometry and scatterometry stand out as powerful techniques for the metrology of thin films and periodic structures with non-destructive approaches.1 As a widely used technique for probing the intricate properties of thin films, EUV reflectometry offers a comprehensive suite of capabilities ranging from reflectance measurement and layer thickness determination to the detailed analysis of multilayer interfaces2 and the precise extraction of optical constants,3, 4 notably the refractive index (n) and the extinction coefficient (k). On the other hand, EUV scatterometry emerges as a potent tool tailored for the assessment of periodic structures, such as gratings, exerting its prowess in geometric parameter analysis,5–7 such as critical dimension (CD), grating pitch, and overlay. Together, these techniques not only enrich our understanding of semiconductor materials and devices but also play a pivotal role in advancing the frontiers of nanotechnology and semiconductor manufacturing.
In this study, we present the results of optical constants (n and k) determination and layer thickness characterization with reflectometry procedure for an EUV attenuated phase shifting photomask blank. Additionally, we analyze scatterometry results for line-space grating profile reconstruction and overlay measurement. These measurements were conducted using the reflective grazing incidence nanoscope for EUV (REGINE) instrument8 at the Swiss Light Source. Through this investigation, we aim to contribute to the advancement of semiconductor metrology techniques and their applications in the semiconductor industry.

Situated within the metrology branch of the XIL-II beamline at the Swiss Light Source, REGINE benefits from a spherical grating monochromator with a spectral bandwidth of λ/∆λ = 1500. This instrument facilitates access to a photon energy range spanning from 80 to 200 eV. The illumination beam, after passing through the monochromator, is focused onto the sample’s surface by a Ruthenium-coated ellipsoidal mirror. The mirror has a reflectance of 93% for the wavelength of 13.5 nm, with the illumination incident at a grazing angle of 6◦. The sample under investigation is mounted vertically on an XYZ linear-stage set, offering an extensive travel range of 31 mm, 21 mm and 51 mm along the X, Y, and Z axes, respectively, with a resolution of 1 nm. Additionally, this stage set is complemented by a rotational stage that enables the adjustment of the incidence angle of the illumination beam, ranging from 0◦ to 28.6◦ in grazing incidence configuration. To capture the reflected light from the sample, an in-vacuum CCD camera (Teledyne PI-MTE2) is co-axially installed on another rotational stage. This stage allows the camera to rotate, facilitating the collection of reflected light data across various angles. Through this meticulously designed setup, REGINE offers a versatile and precise platform for a wide array of metrology experiments, ranging from thin film characterization to detailed grating analysis and CDI of patterned wafers.
3. OPTICAL CONSTANTS AND LAYER THICKNESS CHARACTERIZATION
In this section, we detail the reflectometry procedure employed for the determination of optical constants (n and k) and layer thickness analysis on an EUV attenuated phase shifting photomask blank sample. The sample configuration includes a 33 nm Platinum-based alloy absorber layer,11 a 2.5 nm Ru capping layer, 40 bilayers of Si (4.2 nm) and Mo (2.8 nm) as the multilayer, and a Si substrate.
Initially, the total reflectance of the sample was measured using the REGINE instrument, as depicted in Figure 2a. A reference measurement was first obtained with the beam propagating directly to the CCD camera.

To select the optimal illumination conditions (wavelength and incidence angle) for absorber n and k determination and layer thickness characterization, the beam probing depth in the sample stack was calculated based on the initial sample model for each combination of wavelength and grazing incidence angle. Figure 3a displays the beam probing depth when the incident intensity drops to its 1/e2. The data marked with stars (grazing incidence angle from 2.6◦ to 16.6◦) were selected for absorber n and k determination, as this portion of the data corresponds to less than half of the initial absorber thickness and it is assumed to remain within the absorber layer even though the n and k values are modified during the parameter optimization.
Considering the wavelength-dependent nature of optical constants, for each illumination wavelength, absorber n and k values were independently modified within a certain variation range using a direct-search approach in the simulation as shown in Figure 3b. The simulated reflectance curve was compared to the experimental one, and the optimal case, characterized by the minimum root-mean-square (RMS) between the two curves, was identified, as shown in Figure 4. Reconstructed n and k values were then extracted for each wavelength. Table 1 summarizes the initial absorber material n and k values along with the reconstructed values obtained from REGINE.

Several potential reasons may account for this inaccuracy, such as the exclusion of surface roughness of the gratings in the model and the absence of HSQ n and k values characterized through the previously described reflectometry procedure. An upgrade of the RCWA simulation algorithm is imperative to enhance the performance of overlay measurement.
5. CONCLUSIONS AND OUTLOOK
In conclusion, REGINE demonstrates its versatility in selectively characterizing different layers within stacked samples through reflectometry procedures. In this study, we delved into the investigation of the absorber optical constants (n and k) of an EUV attenuated phase shifting photomask blank, alongside the reconstruction of thicknesses for the absorber, Ru capping layer, and the Ru-Si intermixing layer. Additionally, we showcased scatterometry characterization of a line-space grating, facilitating the reconstruction of the grating profile. However, our attempts at overlay metrology revealed the need for improvement in the simulation algorithm to achieve better performance.
Looking ahead, it is important to cross-check our results with those obtained from commercial tools and transmission electron microscopy (TEM) for validation and further refinement. Moreover, ongoing efforts are directed towards the development of a more robust scatterometry algorithm specifically tailored for overlay metrology. These endeavors will undoubtedly contribute to advancing the capabilities of REGINE and expanding its utility in semiconductor metrology and nanotechnology research.
